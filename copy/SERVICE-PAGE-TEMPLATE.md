## Field spec

**Kicker** — `Parent service · Page name`. Not a heading element; styling only.

**H1** — must contain the primary keyword phrase *and* carry a benefit.
Target 45–65 characters. Exactly one per page. Never put the joke here at the
expense of the keyword; you can have both.

**Sub-deck** — one line under H1, ≤ 95 characters. This is where the wit goes.

**Title tag** — ≤ 60 characters, primary keyword at the front, ` | Pearldrop`
at the end. Must differ from the H1 (title sells the click; H1 confirms the page).

**Meta description** — 140–158 characters. A benefit plus the thing only
Pearldrop offers. Doesn't affect ranking; does affect click-through.

**Body** — 150–220 words, four short paragraphs maximum. Rules:
- Open on the reader's problem, not on Pearldrop.
- At least one *concrete, specific* detail per paragraph — kit, a moment, a
  number, a real constraint. Specificity is the whole voice.
- One British/self-deprecating touch per page. Not more.
- Work the secondary keywords in naturally or leave them out.

**Great for** — exactly 3 items. Each is `Benefit — why it matters`, ≤ 22 words.
Benefits, never features.

**Video examples** — exactly 3, real films from the portfolio, one line each
≤ 20 words. Include the embed URL. Credit the agency if there is one.

**Testimonial** — verbatim from `site-audit/about-us-testimonials.md`, with name
and organisation. Prefer one connected to this service.

**Form** — H2 + one-line sub + button label.

### Banned
"resonate", "seamlessly", "elevate", "unlock", "bespoke", "cutting-edge",
"in today's fast-paced world", "we pride ourselves", "solutions".
British English throughout (-ise, programme, organisation). First person plural.

### 2 — Body

**H2: The tent comes down. The film doesn't.**

You've spent months on this. The build, the samples, the staff shirts, the
weather contingency nobody needed. Then it's Sunday evening, the van's loaded,
and the entire thing exists as about forty phone photos and a slightly damp
tote bag.

That's the bit we fix. We film activations, launches and roadshows so the energy
survives the get-out — the queue that went round the block, the kid who wouldn't
leave the stand, the exact second your product came out of the box. Steadicam
for the movement, timelapse for the scale, and proper sound, because half of
what makes an activation feel alive is the noise it makes.

Got brand ambassadors or live performers? Even better. We'll borrow them for
five minutes before doors open and get something they'd never manage mid-shift
with a queue watching.

And if you need stills too, we shoot both. One crew, one call sheet, one
invoice — instead of two companies elbowing each other inside a 6m × 3m stand.

### 3 — Great for

**Great for:**

- **Making the buzz last** — months of content to post, not one story that vanishes overnight
- **Proving it worked** — something concrete for the board, the client, and next year's sponsors
- **Feeding what's next** — cutdowns for social, the site, the sales deck and the pitch for round two
