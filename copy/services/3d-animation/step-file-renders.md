### 2 — Body

**H2: Your STEP file is already the model. We don't rebuild it.**

A lot of "3D product animation" starts with someone squinting at your
photographs and modelling the thing again from scratch — fillets in roughly the
right place, fastener count roughly right, tolerances nowhere. If you have a
STEP file, or any other CAD export, you've already paid for that geometry once.
We bring it straight into our 3D software as it is.

From there it's a materials and lighting job, and that's where the hours
actually go. Anodised aluminium doesn't behave like powder coat; a brushed
finish needs directional highlights or it reads as flat grey plastic. We build
the surfaces, then light the object in a virtual studio — the same reflectors,
softboxes and sweeps you'd rig for a real product shoot, minus the courier and
the fingerprints.

Then it moves. Slow rotations for a product page, exploded assemblies for a spec
sheet, sectioned housings that show the part doing its job inside. Because the
model is your model, you can label components and the callouts will match the
drawing.

The useful part is timing: renders exist before tooling does. Marketing runs
alongside manufacture instead of waiting for it, and when Rev C shifts a
mounting boss, we re-import rather than re-model.
