### 2 — Body

**H2: The hardest films to make well**

Somebody is about to describe the worst year of their life to a stranger with a
camera. Get that wrong and you have an unusable interview and a person who
feels used. Getting it right is most of the job, and it starts before the kit
comes out of the car — a proper conversation about what will and won't be
asked, who sees the edit first, and the absolute right to stop.

We've been making charity films for more than ten years, for Blesma, Fareshare,
Herts Young Homeless, Age UK Hertfordshire and the East and North Herts
Hospitals Charity. That experience mostly shows up as restraint: one camera
instead of three in a small room, no lighting rig where a window will do, and
an editor who knows the difference between moving an audience and squeezing it.

Different jobs need different films. A 60-second appeal cut for paid social has
to land in the first three seconds. An impact story for funders can breathe for
four minutes. A volunteer recruitment film needs to be honest about the early
starts, or nobody stays past week two.

Charity budgets are charity budgets. We'll tell you plainly what a day's
filming buys, and where a second day genuinely pays for itself.
