### 2 — Body

**H2: BSL is a language, not English with hands**

It has its own grammar and its own word order, and it was recognised in law as a
language of England, Wales and Scotland by the British Sign Language Act 2022.
That matters here for a practical reason: a signed version of your film is a
translation, and translations need a qualified person and proper preparation
time. It is not something to improvise on the day.

So we work only with certified BSL interpreters. They receive your script and
your film in advance, prepare the interpretation so the signing genuinely fits
what is being said, and then perform it to camera against a clean background in
our studio.

We add the result as an overlay to any video — one we produced, or one you send
us. Size and placement are set so the interpreter is large enough to read
comfortably and never sits on top of your captions, your graphics or the thing
the film is actually pointing at.

Captions and a BSL overlay do different jobs, and many of our clients use both.
If you want a view on which your audience needs, ask us. We'd rather have that
conversation early than add signing to a film that was never designed to carry
it.

### 3 — Great for

**Great for:**

- **Reaching deaf audiences properly** — a prepared, certified interpretation rather than captions standing in for a first language
- **Public sector and healthcare communication** — information people rely on, delivered in the language they actually think in
- **Showing inclusion in practice** — a signed film says more about an organisation's values than a statement about them ever will
