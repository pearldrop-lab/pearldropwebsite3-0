### 2 — Body

**H2: The commuter on the 7:42 has the sound off**

So does the person in the open-plan office, the one in bed at 11pm, and most of
the people who'll meet your brand through a social feed. Without subtitles,
every word you paid a voiceover artist to say lands on nobody. The film still
plays. It just says nothing.

Subtitles fix that for everyone at once. They're the single cheapest thing you
can add to a video, and the only one that improves accessibility, watch time and
search all in one go — a transcript gives Google actual text to index, where
before it had a thumbnail and a guess.

There's a compliance argument too, and for a lot of our clients it's the
deciding one. WCAG asks for captions on pre-recorded video at its most basic
level, and public sector bodies in the UK have a legal duty to meet it. Councils,
NHS trusts and colleges don't get to treat captions as a nice-to-have, and
frankly nor should anyone else.

We caption everything we produce, and we'll caption films we didn't make. If you
need languages beyond English, we work with several transcription partners and
will quote on request.
