### 2 — Body

**H2: There's a man in Stevenage waving at a monitor**

Come to our studio on Lytton Way and you may find Tom Fuller, our animator,
gesturing at a screen and grinning to himself. He is not having a moment. He's
performance-capturing a character — using his own movement to drive the shrugs,
the head tilts and the timing, because hand-keyed gestures always look a beat
too neat.

A character can be a person, an animal, a logo or, in one case, an anxious piece
of fruit. They can talk straight down the lens or act out scenes under a
voiceover. What they all do is give your message somewhere to live: an audience
will follow a face through four minutes of information they'd never sit through
as a slide deck.

They earn their keep hardest on the difficult subjects. Debt, bereavement,
safeguarding, leaving home in a hurry — a drawn character can carry that story
without asking a real person to relive it on camera, and without the viewer
spending the whole film reading their face instead of listening.

And once a character exists, it keeps working: same figure across a series, the
induction film, the campaign, the thing you make next spring. No day rate, no
diary clash, no re-shoot when they move jobs.
