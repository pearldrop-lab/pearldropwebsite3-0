### 2 — Body

**H2: Yes, you could write it down. They won't read it.**

You almost certainly already have. Two thousand words on a product page, a
process diagram, a PDF nobody downloads, and a sales team who each explain it a
slightly different way. Meanwhile the person you actually want gives your
homepage about four seconds before deciding whether this is for them.

An animated explainer does the same job visually and out loud at once, which is
why it sticks. It's the right call when your product is abstract, when you need
to show a workflow, when filming isn't practical — try photographing a data
transfer — or when you want the same look every time, no matter who's presenting.

Ours run to a fixed order: discovery, script, storyboard, animation and sound,
then review. The storyboard matters most. You see every scene laid out and sign
it off before a single frame is animated, which is the point at which changes
cost nothing but an email.

You get versions cut for the website, for social, and for the pitch deck that
somebody will inevitably open on a laptop with the sound off. Most clients are
still using theirs three years later, which is more than can be said for the
brochure.
